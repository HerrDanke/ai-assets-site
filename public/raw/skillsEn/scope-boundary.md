Scope boundary is a methodology for explicitly defining what an engineering task "does" and "does not do." It intervenes after the decision map has aligned the direction, transforming directional consensus into executable boundary declarations. The output of a scope boundary typically consists of three parts: a list of deliverables within scope, explicit exclusions outside scope, and guardrail rules to prevent scope creep. Guardrail rules define under what circumstances scope changes can be accepted, under what circumstances they must be rejected, and the approval process for changes.

Scope creep is one of the common causes of engineering task loss of control, and scope boundaries counteract this tendency through "explicit exclusion." Simply saying "we will do A" is not enough; you must also simultaneously say "we explicitly will not do B and C." This explicit boundary gives the team a yardstick for making trade-off judgments during subsequent execution: when a new requirement is proposed, comparing it against the boundary allows for a quick determination of whether it is core or noise. A scope boundary is not set in stone, but any change must undergo explicit evaluation rather than being absorbed by default.
