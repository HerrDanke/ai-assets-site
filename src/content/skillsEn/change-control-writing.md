Change control document writing is the process of transforming change requests into formal documents that comply with GMP requirements. In this phase, the writer breaks down the change intent into a set of structured sections, ensuring that the document covers all compliance key points and can pass the quality department's review. A change control document not only records the content of the change itself, but also the rationale, impact, risks, and verification requirements, providing a complete chain of evidence for the approval, implementation, and closure of the change.

A high-quality change control document is not only the basis for change implementation, but also critical evidence for GMP compliance. It needs to answer core questions such as "why change", "what to change", "how to change", "how to verify", and "how to roll back" — specific implementation details are left to the execution team to elaborate in the implementation plan. Writing change control documents requires integrating risk assessment methods such as FMEA to ensure that the risks of the change are fully identified and controlled. The final change control document serves as the raw material for the change approval process and as important evidence for subsequent audits and inspections.

## Document Structure

A change control document must include the following sections:

1. **Objective** — Purpose of the change, defined precisely in one sentence
2. **Scope** — Clearly define the scope of the change, without ambiguity
3. **Background / Justification** — Rationale for the change (business-driven / compliance-driven / CAPA-driven)
4. **Description of Change** — Detailed description of the change (Before → After)
5. **Impact Assessment** — Impact assessment, must cover:
   - GMP compliance
   - Product quality
   - Data integrity
   - Validation status (whether it affects existing validation)
   - Related systems and processes
6. **Risk Assessment** — Risk assessment (using FMEA or equivalent methods)
7. **Implementation Plan** — Implementation plan (clear steps, executable, including timeline)
8. **Verification / Validation Requirements** — Verification/validation requirements
9. **Rollback Plan** — Rollback strategy (if applicable)
10. **Conclusion** — Conclusion

## Key Compliance Points

- Any change that affects a validated status must trigger a revalidation assessment
- Changes must be evaluated for their impact on 21 CFR Part 11 compliance
- Confirmation of all prerequisites must be completed before change implementation
- Closure confirmation must be performed after change completion

## Writing Rules

- Change descriptions must be precise to the level of specific parameters / configurations / versions being changed
- Impact Assessment must not be generic; it must analyze item by item for the specific change
- Risk Assessment must have clear acceptance criteria
- Implementation plans must include responsibility assignment and completion indicators
